English Wikipedia pageviews, 1,905 deaths and 175 awards, 2016 to 2025

Fame half-life

How fast the world stops looking someone up.

Half the extra attention a death brings is gone within 1 day of the peak. A year later the page still draws 1.08 times its old traffic (median of 1,905 deaths). Awards fade just as fast at first, and where they settle depends on how known the winner was: Nobel laureates keep 8.78 times their old traffic, Oscar winners 0.38 times.

The dataset

The fade

The spike is gone within days; a residue stays

On the median death the page draws 200 times its usual traffic on the peak day, which is the day after the death in 43% of cases. The averaged curve loses half its excess in 1 day and nine tenths in 5 days, and sits at 1.96 times baseline a month later and 1.09 times in the last month of the year. Awards peak at 817 times and lose nine tenths in 4 days. Deaths jump again on day +365, the anniversary; awards climb in the last week of the year as the next year's prize comes round.

The curve

Two exponentials fit better than one, or a power law

Three curves were fitted to each event's excess views after the peak: an exponential, a power law, and the biexponential (a sum of two exponentials) that Candia et al. (2019) found in the decay of attention to songs, films and biographies over years. The biexponential has the lowest AIC for 91% of 1,808 deaths and 78% of 139 awards, with a median margin of 25 AIC points over the power law. Its fast part has a median time constant of 5.4 days; the slow part's is 1,418 days, so long that within a year it is close to a flat step above the old baseline.

The aftermath

A death leaves a page busier than before; an Oscar leaves it quieter

A year after a death the median page draws 1.08 times its old traffic, and 59% of pages sit above their baseline. Awards spread far wider. Nobel laureates, with a median of 20 views a day beforehand, keep 8.78 times that. Academy Award winners start from 15,120 a day, already raised by awards season, and end the year at 0.38 times it. Among deaths, each extra year of age at death goes with 2.0% less lift (p < 0.001), and pages that were busier beforehand gain less.

Who lasts

Which prize matters more than what the person did

Among deaths, the share of the first year's extra views that arrive after the first week runs from 23% (business) to 33% (writing and journalism), and median lift from 0.97 times (business) to 1.22 times (music). The gap between Nobel and Oscar winners is wider than any gap between occupations.

Limits

What this cannot show

  • English Wikipedia only. A view is someone looking a person up, which is not the same as caring about them.
  • The fame floor of 30 Wikidata sitelinks decides who counts as famous and leans toward people known in many countries. It is today's count, so some people clear it because of the death itself.
  • Some automated traffic passes the user filter. Capping any day above 50 times its neighbors changes 31 events and 1 half-life; median lift is 1.08 for deaths with the cap.
  • A death or an award can coincide with other news about the same person, such as a film release or a scandal, and nothing in the data separates the two.
  • For the Academy Awards the baseline window falls in awards season, after the nominations, so the "before" traffic is already raised. That is part of why awards settle below their baseline.
  • The half-life as specified saturates: the spike is narrower than the 7-day smoothing window, so the half-life is one day for 95% of deaths. With a 3-day window the median is 2 days, with 14 days 1. The share of extra views after week one, and the fitted curves, carry the comparisons instead.
